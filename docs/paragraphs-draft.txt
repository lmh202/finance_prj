**Daily Strategy Engine.** The strategy engine reads price history and nothing else: momentum over 20 and 60 days, price against its 50- and 200-day moving averages, rolling volatility, RSI, drawdown, and beta. From these it classifies the market into one of **four regimes — bullish, bearish, high-volatility, or sideways** — and ranks every holding cross-sectionally against the others. **Its contribution to the fusion engine is direction, and direction alone: it is the only component in the entire system permitted to say buy or sell, and it fixes 100% of the relative weights between stocks.** That ranking is converted into expected return through a Grinold-Kahn scaling, `alpha = IC × σ_horizon × z(direction)`, with the information coefficient set to a deliberately conservative **0.02** — a tenth of what the previous hardcoded value implied, and the most we could justify from our own measurements. What Strategy never decides is how much capital stands behind its opinion.

**News Intelligence Engine.** The news engine runs continuously: RSS feeds are collected and normalised, duplicates are collapsed by URL and title similarity, and at most five essential events per day are surfaced and mapped to the holdings that they actually concern. Sentiment is scored by **a local FinBERT — a BERT encoder fine-tuned on financial text — which runs fully offline from cached weights and calls no external API.** Its contribution to the fusion engine is the one most often misunderstood: **news never casts a directional vote. It reaches the decision through exactly one channel — as an exogenous news-attention term inside the volatility forecast — so it changes how large a position is, never whether to buy or sell it.** A degraded or stale feed is reported as degraded rather than silently treated as an observed quiet market, so a broken RSS source is visible instead of resembling calm conditions.

**Risk Engine.** The risk engine is where everything the news desk files actually lands. **HAR-X forecasts volatility** from log realised volatility over 5, 22 and 66 sessions, a Parkinson high-low range estimator, and the news-attention term, and **Filtered Historical Simulation** then converts that forecast into downside risk using empirical quantiles of standardised returns — fat-tailed, left-skewed, and calibrated on data through 2020 only, **so no normal distribution is assumed anywhere**. An EWMA correlation matrix carries the same forecasts up to portfolio level. **Its contribution to the fusion engine is everything Strategy is not allowed to touch: per-stock position size, gross exposure, the cash weight, and the full covariance matrix the optimiser solves against.** It is also the **only model in the project we ever promoted to production** — adding causal news attention improved five-session out-of-sample QLIKE by 5.24%, with a 95% bootstrap interval of +2.38% to +8.83% and Diebold-Mariano p = 0.0011, and its VaR-95 breach rate backtests at 4.32% against a 5% target.
